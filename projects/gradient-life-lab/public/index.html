The rules of this world

Each cell holds food 0–10, plant mass 0–10, and at most one animal of size 1–10, plus a dormant spore occupying 1 unit when present. Their sum never exceeds 11. Fractional quantities let changes happen gradually.

Touching plant cells (including diagonals) form a shared nutrient network. Networks merge on contact and split when feeding or withering removes a connecting cell. Each tick the network consumes 0.01 food per unit of plant mass for maintenance. Unmet maintenance starts the starvation clock; after the grace period cells wither by 0.15 per tick.

Fed networks of mass 1.5+ can extend one 0.2-mass thread per tick toward the strongest food signal, transferring biomass from an established cell. All connected cells can use food anywhere in their network to grow, up to the growth rate per occupied cell. Sharing is instantaneous in this prototype; there is no transport cost.

Food diffuses through four orthogonal neighbors down concentration differences. The rate controls the fraction exchanged, divided among four directions. Transfers obey food and cell capacity limits and conserve food. Diffusion can be disabled independently of plant networks. Renewal adds new food and starts at 0.01 per cell per tick.

Animals eat up to 0.15 × size from the richest plant cell in reach (their own or a neighbor). Eating adds energy; upkeep costs energy × size. Surplus permits growth. Starving animals shrink, then die at size 1. Animals that find no meal move toward plant signals, or wander on ties, only where they fit.

Plant reproduction: a connected group (including diagonal contacts) needs at least four cells to persist for two qualifying ticks; on the third, it replaces one mature plant cell with a permanent spore. That cell must have at least 1 plant mass; the spore occupies 1 capacity unit, replacing all plant mass there. A successful reproduction resets the network’s timer. Spores are not edible, do not wither or move, and block plant extension into their cell. Food can diffuse through their remaining capacity. At the next tick start, a spore becomes a size-1 plant if its own cell has food and no animal, and all eight available neighboring cells have no plant, animal or spore. Food does not block germination. Off-board neighbors are empty.

Grazing deposits a local damage signal equal to plant mass eaten (capped at 10). It retains 80% per tick and influences growth on the next tick. Plant tips rank food attraction minus damage avoidance: weight 6 times the sum of recent damage divided by distance within sensing range. This biases extension away from grazing even if the damaged cell is completely eaten. Weight zero disables the response. Red cell outlines show recent damage. This is a damage-response rule, not a claim of conscious pain.

Crowding overrides feeding: with one or more occupied neighboring cells at the start of a tick, an animal tries to dart up to three adjacent steps along a passable path. It favors fewer neighboring animals, then lower surrounding animal signals, then plant availability. Every traversed cell must have room for its full size. It can feed at its destination but cannot take a second normal move. If no less crowded destination is reachable, it stays and feeds normally. Threshold 9 disables escape. Escape has normal upkeep, with no extra movement energy cost.

Animals age once per tick. At age 150 they stop dividing; from age 220 they have a 25% chance each tick of losing 0.25 size. Death occurs below size 1. Declining animals can eat and move but cannot regain size. Daughters start at age zero and first age on their next tick. Decline starts no earlier than the reproductive cutoff; set its chance to zero to disable age-related shrinking. Lost mass is not recycled.

After five complete ticks at size 10, an animal has a 10% chance each tick of dividing if a neighboring cell can fit a daughter. The parent is replaced by two daughters, each 30% of its size (size 3) and stored energy. The remaining 40% is a division cost, not recycled food. Daughters have new identities, start with zero full-size ticks, and first act on the following tick. Falling below size 10 resets the waiting period. Set the chance to zero to disable division.

Signals use the post-diffusion snapshot. Diffusion happens first, then plant network actions and animal actions resolve in seeded shuffled order, so collisions have reproducible outcomes without directional priority. Animal movement boundaries are open: a passable escape path may cross an edge within its step limit, removing that animal and counting a departure. Ordinary movement can also leave when no visible food offers a better choice. Plants and nutrient diffusion remain bounded; standing at an edge does not itself cause removal. No predation or nutrient recycling yet.

Initial conditions (revised to give plants access to nutrients): 32 food cells at 10, 12 plant cells at 1 rooted in food cells, seven animals at size 1 and energy 3. “Fully exhausted” is interpreted as fully grown plant mass; depletion is tracked separately.

An exploration of behavior before cognition. No learning, memory, or self-model is built in.
